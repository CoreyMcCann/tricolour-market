# References

## Fictional demonstration

Tricolour Market, Tricolour+, the customer, all prices and all billing dates are fictional. This is an independent CISC 497 student project. The obstructive route adapts historical design patterns. The direct route is the group’s proposed redesign, with a visible control, an optional offer, and a clear account and billing outcome. The diagrams show the implemented mock routes and make no claim about measured effectiveness.

## FTC amended complaint against Amazon.com, Inc.

September 20, 2023. PDF pages 47–55, historical desktop Iliad flow (¶¶127–155).

[Source](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2023-09-20-067-AmendedComplaint%28redacted%29.pdf#page=47)

The FTC alleged that the historical desktop Iliad process took four pages, at least six clicks and presented fifteen options. We adapt the management menu, a benefits reminder, an offer and repeated decisions. These are allegations about a historical interface. The fictional routes use their own screens, offers and terms.

## FTC Secures Historic $2.5 Billion Settlement Against Amazon

September 25, 2025. Press release, monetary judgment and changes to Prime.

[Source](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-secures-historic-25-billion-settlement-against-amazon)

The FTC reports a settlement addressing both Prime enrollment and cancellation. The total combines a $1 billion civil penalty and $1.5 billion in consumer redress. The settlement provides case context for the historical complaint.

## Sheil et al., Staying at the Roach Motel: Cross-Country Analysis of Manipulative Subscription and Cancellation Flows

CHI 2024 (preprint revised March 14, 2024). §§3.3, 4.2.2, 4.3.2, 4.4.2, 4.6 and 5.1.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17145v3)

The researchers document obstacles such as extra decisions and offers, and discuss examples of prominent cancellation controls and clear confirmations. The findings describe the news sites and personas studied at the time. They inform our comparison without measuring the effect of our redesign.

## Which?, Dark Patterns 2026

November 5, 2026. Methodology and Findings, “Subscription traps” and Figure 5.

[Source](https://www.which.co.uk/policy-and-insight/article/dark-patterns-2026-aCroI9S4updG)

In this self-reported UK survey, 29% of respondents who had tried to cancel or change a subscription in the preceding year found cancellation difficult. Figure 5 reports barriers for that subgroup (769 respondents). This evidence provides UK context and does not estimate Canadian or global experience.
